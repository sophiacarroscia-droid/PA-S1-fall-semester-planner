Your brain isn't bad at pharm — drug-name recall is one of the toughest ADHD tasks that exists (arbitrary words with no built-in logic). Seminar in Diagnostic Test Interpretation

Matarazzo · 1 credit · Mon 1:00–1:50

Flowchart: how to approach any diagnostic test question

  1. What's the pretest probability? (history + exam first, always)
  2. Sensitivity vs specificity — do you need to rule IN or rule OUT?
  3. SnNout: a highly sensitive test, when negative, rules out.
  4. SpPin: a highly specific test, when positive, rules in.
  5. Apply likelihood ratios if given — LR+>10 or LR-<0.1 = strong shift in probability
  6. Match the test to cost, invasiveness, and turnaround before picking "next best step"

This is a 1-credit seminar — low credit load, but the reasoning skill it teaches shows up inside almost every other exam this semester. Don't deprioritize it just because it's light on paper.

Grit & Motivation

Straight from your program's own orientation — and a few words for you specifically.

For you, specifically:

You're not starting this semester from zero. You're starting it from a full year of proof — A's, B's, and one C that's already told you exactly where to put extra energy. That is not a person who is bad at this. That is a person who is one adjusted study system away from a much better fall. You have ADHD, and that means the standard "just study more" advice was never going to work for you — so we're not using it. Structure, spaced repetition, movement breaks (lift, Creami, whatever resets you), and a plan that catches you when you fall off it — that's the actual plan. Hard days are part of the data, not the verdict. Keep going.

How "gritty" are you? (from orientation)

In PA school, GRIT isn't about being perfect or never struggling — it's about how you respond when things get hard. PA training is challenging: heavy coursework, frequent exams, uncertainty in clinical learning, and emotional stress. GRIT helps you stay committed to becoming a competent, ethical provider, even when things feel overwhelming. GRIT is developed, not fixed — reflection, effective time management, help-seeking, self-care, and a growth mindset all strengthen it. Every challenge in PA school is a chance to build the perseverance you'll rely on throughout your clinical career.

Reflection prompts (yours to answer, no pressure)

  • How did you respond to challenges this summer, emotionally and behaviorally?
  • What actions demonstrated (or limited) your perseverance and professionalism?
  • How did you use feedback, self-reflection, or support systems to move forward?
  • If faced with a similar situation again, what would you do differently to strengthen your grit?

Your commitment this week

You are not failing because it is hard. You are growing.

Non-negotiables, PA-school style

Studying for Friday's exam goes on the calendar first. Sleep is non-negotiable — it doesn't get traded for late-night cramming. Lifting and Creami time count as protected self-care, not indulgences — performance and wellness are linked, and rest improves learning efficiency. A social event is the flexible thing, not sleep or your study blocks.
